# Venture #1: MahuaShilp: Canopy-Net & NIR Rotary Micro-Dryer
**Sector**: Agritech & Forest Value Chain  
**Prototype Budget**: ₹7,800 (₹7,000 - ₹9,000) | **Complexity**: Medium  
**Composite Viability Score**: 9.39/10.0

### 1. Executive Pitch
> Eliminating ground fires and dirt contamination in Bastar's 50,000-ton Mahua harvest through canopy nets and solar-thermal NIR rotary micro-dryers, doubling tribal farmgate realization.

### 2. Problem Statement
- **Target Population & Geography**: Over 350,000 tribal families across Bastar Division who collect Mahua flowers (Madhuca longifolia) during March-May.
- **Frequency & Occurrence**: Annual 6-8 week intensive harvest cycle.
- **Why Current Solutions Fail**: Gatherers deliberately burn dry Sal leaf litter beneath Mahua trees so fallen greenish-cream flowers can be spotted on ash. This triggers 60%+ of Bastar forest fires. Fallen flowers pick up soil, animal feces, and silica, while sun-drying on dirt tracks ferments sugars and breeds mycotoxins. Open sun-drying takes 4-5 days.
- **Consequences if Unsolved**: Destruction of forest biodiversity, severe respiratory hazard from smoke, and loss of 40-50% in farmgate selling price due to low grade (sold at ₹30-35/kg vs ₹70-80/kg for food-grade hygienic flowers).
- **Evidence & Ground Citations**:
  - TRIFED Minor Forest Produce Annual Report 2024
  - Chhattisgarh State Minor Forest Produce Federation (CG MFP) Procurement Data
  - Bastar Forest Division Fire Incident Reports (March-May 2023-2025)

### 3. Solution Specification
A dual-stage system: (1) A lightweight ground-elevated tensioned poly-mesh canopy net that catches falling Mahua flowers without soil contact, eliminating leaf burning, and (2) A portable solar-assisted rotary drum micro-dryer with NIR moisture monitoring that cures flowers to <12% moisture in 6 hours.

**Hardware Architecture**:
- Telescopic bamboo/aluminum perimeter frame
- UV-stabilized 40-mesh monofilament netting
- Perforated stainless steel rotary drum
- 12V 40W DC geared drive motor
- PTC heating element with solar-thermal air pre-heater collector
- ESP32 microcontroller
- NIR reflectance optical sensor

**Software & Firmware Stack**:
- Embedded PID thermal/moisture control firmware
- Offline drying profile logger
- Low-power BLE diagnostic app

**Edge AI / IoT & Algorithms**:
- NIR spectral reflectance regression for moisture estimation
- Adaptive PID drying curve algorithm to prevent caramelization of natural sugars

### 4. Technical Architecture & End-to-End Pipeline
- **Input Stage**: Canopy catch net deployed around Mahua trunk; flowers drop directly onto clean mesh; gathered in clean bins without ground touching.
- **Processing Stage**: Flowers loaded into rotary drum micro-dryer; drum rotates at 12 RPM under regulated warm air stream (42°C-45°C).
- **Decision Stage**: Embedded NIR sensor measures water absorption band (1450nm) at regular drum cycles; micro-controller throttles airflow and drum speed.
- **Action Stage**: Automated buzzer and visual green LED activates when moisture reaches 11.5% target; heating cuts off to prevent over-drying.
- **Output Stage**: Golden-yellow, silica-free food-grade Mahua stored in hermetic bags; ready for premium brewery and confectionery sale.

```text
[Falling Flowers] -> [Elevated Net] -> [Clean Hopper] -> [Rotary Drum (45°C)] -> [NIR Moisture Sensor] -> [Food-Grade Dry Mahua]
```

### 5. Target Users, Buyers & Beneficiaries
- **Operational Users**: Tribal women self-help groups (SHGs) and forest-dwelling families gathering Mahua.
- **Economic Buyers**: Van Dhan Vikas Kendras (VDVKs), Large-sized Adivasi Multipurpose Societies (LAMPS), TRIFOOD Jagdalpur, and private food/craft beverage processors.
- **Societal Beneficiaries**: Over 350,000 forest gatherers, Bastar Forest Department (fire reduction), and premium food/beverage consumers.

### 6. Market Opportunity (Bottom-Up Analysis)
- **Total Addressable Market (TAM)**: ₹2,400 Crore ($290M) national Mahua trade across Chhattisgarh, MP, Odisha, Jharkhand, and Maharashtra.
- **Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)**: ₹450 Crore ($55M) organized Mahua procurement and processing market in Chhattisgarh.
- **Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM - 3 Year)**: ₹28 Crore ($3.4M) serviceable market in Bastar Division across 240 Van Dhan Vikas Kendras over 3 years.
- **Derivation Methodology**: Bottom-up: 50,000 MT Bastar harvest x ₹70/kg food-grade value = ₹350 Cr regional potential; 240 VDVKs each procuring 10 micro-dryers @ ₹25,000 = ₹6 Cr hardware + netting supplies.

### 7. Business Model & Revenue Mechanics
- **Who Pays**: VDVKs funded through TRIFED/DMF grants, CG MFP Federation, and FPOs.
- **What They Pay For**: Hardware kit purchase (Micro-dryer + 5 Canopy Nets) and replacement mesh consumable packs.

# Venture #2: KodoClean: Centrifugal De-Huller & Air-Classifier for Small Millets
**Sector**: Agritech & Food Processing  
**Prototype Budget**: ₹11,800 (₹10,000 - ₹13,000) | **Complexity**: Medium-High  
**Composite Viability Score**: 9.2/10.0

### 1. Executive Pitch
> A low-power (0.5 HP) centrifugal rubber-rotor de-huller and vibratory air-classifier engineered specifically for tiny Kodo and Kutki grains, cutting broken grain losses from 35% to under 6%.

### 2. Problem Statement
- **Target Population & Geography**: Over 120,000 small-millet (Kodo & Kutki) farmers across Bastar, Sukma, and Kanker districts, and regional millet processing units.
- **Frequency & Occurrence**: Post-harvest processing cycle across 6 months annually.
- **Why Current Solutions Fail**: Chhattisgarh has declared itself the 'Millet Hub of India', but small millets (Kutki grain diameter ~1.5mm, Kodo ~2.0mm) have hard silicified 5-layer husks. Existing rice mills or heavy abrasive emery hullers crush Kutki, causing 30-40% grain breakage into unmarketable flour. Industrial millet mills cost ₹5-12 Lakhs and need 15 HP three-phase industrial power, unavailable in interior tribal hamlets.
- **Consequences if Unsolved**: Tribal farmers are forced to sell raw unhulled millets at MSP (₹30-40/kg) rather than processed edible grain (₹120-160/kg), losing 300% value capture.
- **Evidence & Ground Citations**:
  - ICAR-IIMR (Indian Institute of Millets Research) Small Millet Processing Bulletin
  - Chhattisgarh State Millet Mission Progress Report 2024
  - Bastar District Agriculture Department Millet Baseline Survey

### 3. Solution Specification
A single-phase / solar-compatible 0.5 HP centrifugal impact de-huller with variable-gap vulcanized elastomer impact rings, integrated with a counter-current vibratory pneumatic air-classifier that separates husk from whole grains in a single continuous pass.

**Hardware Architecture**:
- 0.5 HP BLDC 24V motor
- Conical polyurethane rotor with radial acceleration channels
- Replaceable vulcanized silicone-rubber impact stator ring
- Eccentric vibratory screen deck (dual sieve 1.2mm / 1.8mm)
- Aspiration cyclone fan with transparent husk chamber
- ESP32-S3 motor controller with load current sensing

**Software & Firmware Stack**:
- Adaptive motor RPM controller based on moisture and grain variety preset (Kodo vs Kutki vs Ragi)
- Digital throughput logger

**Edge AI / IoT & Algorithms**:
- Current-signature analysis to prevent rotor choking and automatically regulate grain feeding hopper gate via stepper motor

### 4. Technical Architecture & End-to-End Pipeline
- **Input Stage**: Raw unhulled Kutki/Kodo poured into top vibrating feed hopper; stepper-actuated gate regulates flow.
- **Processing Stage**: Grains drop into high-speed centrifugal conical rotor; flung tangentially against resilient rubber impact ring; husk shears off due to differential deceleration without shattering the inner starch kernel.
- **Decision Stage**: Current sensor monitors BLDC load torque; adjusts feeder vibration to maintain peak 25 kg/hr throughput.
- **Action Stage**: Mixture drops across vibratory sieves while an upward cyclonic airflow lifts light husks into collection cyclone.
- **Output Stage**: Whole polished edible millet grain discharges into clean chute; broken grain <6%; husks collected separately for bio-briquettes.

```text
[Unhulled Millet] -> [Auto-Feed Gate] -> [Centrifugal Rubber Rotor] -> [Pneumatic Air Lift] -> [Vibratory Deck] -> [Intact Whole Grain]
```

### 5. Target Users, Buyers & Beneficiaries
- **Operational Users**: Village SHG women operators, tribal youth rural entrepreneurs, and primary cooperative societies.
- **Economic Buyers**: Chhattisgarh Millet Mission, District Agriculture Offices, TRIFED, FPOs, and rural SHG federations.
- **Societal Beneficiaries**: Smallholder millet farming households who retain 70%+ processing margins locally.

### 6. Market Opportunity (Bottom-Up Analysis)
- **Total Addressable Market (TAM)**: ₹1,800 Crore national small-millet processing machinery and service market.
- **Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)**: ₹320 Crore market across tribal millet zones in Chhattisgarh, Odisha, and MP.
- **Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM - 3 Year)**: ₹18 Crore serviceable market targeting 600 village panchayats and FPOs in Bastar division over 3 years.
- **Derivation Methodology**: Bottom-up: 600 tribal panchayats in Bastar Division; 1 village-level mill per 2 panchayats = 300 units @ ₹45,000 = ₹1.35 Cr initial hardware + ₹3.5 Cr annual processing tolling fees.

### 7. Business Model & Revenue Mechanics
- **Who Pays**: FPOs, Agriculture Department via Millet Mission subsidies, and rural micro-entrepreneurs using Mudra/PMEGP loans.
- **What They Pay For**: Complete de-hulling machine, wear-and-tear rubber liner replacement packs, and annual maintenance.
- **Pricing Model**: ₹42,000 per complete machine with solar inverter compatibility; replacement rubber ring kit ₹850.
- **Hardware Revenue Stream**: Machine unit sales with 34% margin.
- **Recurring Revenue Stream**: High-wear elastomer stator rings (replaced every 500 operating hours) + spares.
- **Classification**: B2B / B2G

### 8. Competitive Benchmarking
| Competitor / Alternative | Category | Strengths | Weaknesses | Our Defensible Advantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conventional Abrasive Emery Stone Mill | Incumbent commercial machine | Cheap initial cost (₹25,000), widely available. | Destroys 35-45% of tiny Kutki grains into dust; stones wear down adding grit to food; requires 3-phase 5 HP power. | Elastomer impact de-hulling reduces breakage to <6%; operates on 0.5 HP solar/single-phase power; zero stone grit contamination. |

# Venture #3: BodaShield: Evaporative-Chilled N2 Pod for Wild Mushrooms
**Sector**: Cold Chain & Perishables  
**Prototype Budget**: ₹9,500 (₹8,500 - ₹11,000) | **Complexity**: High  
**Composite Viability Score**: 9.0/10.0

### 1. Executive Pitch
> A portable solar-battery backpack preservation pod combining Peltier-zeolite chilling and micro-nitrogen purging, extending the transit shelf-life of Bastar's ₹2,000/kg wild Boda mushrooms from 24 hours to 8 days.

### 2. Problem Statement
- **Target Population & Geography**: Forest tribals who forage wild Boda (Termitomyces spp.) mushrooms under Sal trees, and regional fresh-produce traders.
- **Frequency & Occurrence**: Intense 4-week window during initial monsoon rains (June-July).
- **Why Current Solutions Fail**: Boda is an underground mycorrhizal mushroom that is considered an exquisite culinary delicacy, fetching ₹1,200 to ₹2,200/kg in Jagdalpur and Raipur. However, Boda has extreme post-harvest cellular respiration. Within 24-36 hours, it sweats, turns black, liquefies, and rots. Ice packs freeze-burn the outer cuticle, while conventional cold rooms are miles away from dense forest collection points.
- **Consequences if Unsolved**: Tribal gatherers are forced to distress-sell Boda to intermediaries for ₹300-400/kg; over 60% of harvested Boda spoils before reaching high-paying urban markets like Raipur, Hyderabad, or Delhi.
- **Evidence & Ground Citations**:
  - ICAR-DMR (Directorate of Mushroom Research) Post-Harvest Bulletin
  - Bastar Mandi Price Bulletin (June-July 2024)
  - Journal of Forest Products and Tribal Economy Research on Termitomyces

### 3. Solution Specification
A ruggedized, insulated 15 kg backpack pod featuring active thermoelectric (Peltier) chilling maintained at 6°C, integrated with an on-demand zeolite micro-pressure swing adsorption (PSA) cartridge that flushes the chamber with 95% nitrogen, halting enzymatic browning and microbial respiration.

**Hardware Architecture**:
- Vacuum-insulated panel / EPP composite backpack enclosure
- Dual TEC1-12706 Peltier cooling modules with heat-pipe sinks
- 12V 20Ah LiFePO4 battery pack with 40W foldable solar panel
- Miniature 12V diaphragm air pump with synthetic zeolite 13X column
- Sensirion SHT31 Temp/RH sensor
- NDIR CO2 and O2 sensor
- ESP32 telemetry board with BLE

**Software & Firmware Stack**:
- Controlled atmosphere preservation firmware
- BLE mobile app showing real-time freshness index and chamber parameters
- GPS breadcrumb logger for foraging traceability

**Edge AI / IoT & Algorithms**:
- Kinetic respiration degradation model predicting remaining shelf-life hours based on time-temperature-gas integral

### 4. Technical Architecture & End-to-End Pipeline
- **Input Stage**: Freshly dug Boda mushrooms gently brushed and loaded into breathable food-grade silicone pod trays.
- **Processing Stage**: Lid sealed; user presses 'Purge'; miniature pump draws ambient air through zeolite column, exhausting O2 and flooding chamber with dry N2 until O2 < 3%.
- **Decision Stage**: Temperature sensor triggers Peltier circuit to maintain stable 6°C (+/- 1°C); RH maintained at 85% without condensation.
- **Action Stage**: Low-power micro-controller sleeps, waking every 10 minutes to verify gas hermeticity and temperature stability.

> A gentle low-temperature (under 40°C) solar-thermal rotary vacuum thin-film dehydrator for Bastar's wild forest honey, reducing moisture from 25% to FSSAI-compliant <18% without destroying bioactive enzymes or darkening color.

### 2. Problem Statement
- **Target Population & Geography**: Over 15,000 tribal honey hunters and Van Dhan Vikas Kendras (VDVKs) gathering wild forest honey (Apis dorsata) in Bastar.
- **Frequency & Occurrence**: Two main harvesting cycles: Summer (Chaitra) and Post-Monsoon (Kartik).
- **Why Current Solutions Fail**: Bastar wild forest honey (harvested from giant rock bees, Apis dorsata) is prized for rich medicinal properties and unique floral nectar. However, fresh wild honey harvested in tropical forests has an inherently high moisture content (23% to 26%). According to FSSAI standards, commercial honey must have moisture <20% (ideally <18%); above 20%, natural osmotolerant wild yeasts ferment the honey into sour alcohol and vinegar within 3 months, bursting containers. Existing commercial honey processing plants use high-temperature thermal pasteurizers (heating honey to 65°C-75°C). This ruins raw forest honey: it destroys delicate heat-sensitive enzymes (diastase, invertase), spikes toxic HMF (hydroxymethylfurfural) levels, and turns golden wild honey black, destroying its premium export value.
- **Consequences if Unsolved**: Tribal gatherers are forced to sell fresh honey at distress prices (₹150-180/kg); millions of liters ferment and spoil; loss of export market access.
- **Evidence & Ground Citations**:
  - FSSAI Honey Quality and Purity Standards Regulations
  - TRIFED Bastar Wild Honey Cluster Processing Studies
  - National Bee Board (NBB) Post-Harvest Honey Management Bulletin

### 3. Solution Specification
A decentralized, village-level low-temperature rotary vacuum falling-film honey dehydrator. Powered by solar-thermal hot water and a 12V DC diaphragm vacuum pump, it boils off excess water vapor under deep vacuum (-0.85 bar) at a gentle 38°C to 40°C (well below the 45°C enzyme denaturing threshold). A rotating stainless-steel disc rotor continuously spreads honey into ultra-thin liquid films inside the vacuum chamber, accelerating moisture extraction down to 17.5% in 2 hours while preserving 100% of live enzymes, natural pollen, and delicate floral aromas.
